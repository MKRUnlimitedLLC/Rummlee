Your role02

The product in one page

A neighbor pays before anyone drives. The handoff is at a store, not at a house on a public card.

  1. A seller opens a Neighborhood sale. Online days stay open. In-person hours are optional. The public card shows hours, never a street.
  2. They set an asking price and a lowest price. The lowest price is hidden.
  3. A buyer pays asking, or makes one offer. Each side gets one counteroffer. A decline ends it.
  4. The money is held until both people confirm the handoff. In beta that money is test credits.
  5. Official store first. Public place second. In person only if the seller turned it on, and only after payment.

The words we use

Neighborhood sale. Handoff. Official store handoff. Public place handoff. In-person handoff.

We do not say porch, driveway, meet at my house, or garage-sale app. We do not ship. A couch, anything over 50 pounds, or anything not in an outer box is in person only. A store is not a warehouse for furniture.

Marketed hardest to women in cities and suburbs, because that is who will not publish a house number. The marketplace is for everyone.

The bet04

A sale, in order

Online does not turn off because the tables are out.

What the seller sets

  • Dates. A sale cannot run longer than 14 days.
  • Online window. Default is Tuesday through Thursday. Offers and pay-asking stay open.
  • Live hours, optional. Neighbors see the days and a rough distance. The address appears after they pay.
  • If they want, a note that a paid item can be picked up at the sale itself.
  • Which handoffs they offer. Official is the default. They can also turn on public, and in person for the live hours.

When something sells online during the sale

  • It is held. Nobody else can buy it.
  • The seller takes it off the table and sets it aside.
  • Only after that do they tap “I set this aside.” The card then says sold online.
  • A walk-up cannot pay cash in the app. If it sold outside Rummlee, the seller marks “sold outside.” That ends the listing and does not move money we never held.

Asking price is public. Lowest price is not, except a +++ member can reveal it up to five times a month. One offer, one counteroffer, one decline. A decline ends the deal. Pay asking stays available the whole time the item is live.

Operating a sale05

The handoff

Two different codes. No names. One box at a time.

Seller arrives

The account code is on their phone and stays hidden until they tap it. It hides again after 20 seconds. They wrote a short code on the box. That short code is not sitting in the buyer’s app.

The counter scans the phone, then checks the code on that box. A match means the store is holding that package.

Buyer arrives

A different account code, same sale. The screen lists the short codes to pull. The buyer types the code on the box they are holding. Only that box is confirmed.

Two items from the same seller are one trip and two boxes. Same account scan. Each box still has to be confirmed.

After that

The seller is not paid for 48 hours. In that window the buyer can report a problem. A camera reads a code and does not save the picture. The counter never shows a name, a price, or an address.

Seller has 2 days to drop a box. Buyer has 5 days to pick it up. After that the buyer is refunded.

In person, the same idea: both people confirm with a scan or a 6-character code. The meetup note appears only after payment. A photo of the account code is the same as handing over the phone.

Chain of custody06

What the store is, and is not

The store holds a box. The store does not run the marketplace.

The associate does

  • Scan a phone. Type the short code on the label.
  • Put the box on a shelf. Pull it when the buyer’s code is scanned.
  • Refuse a package that is unsafe, illegal, or obviously not the item. A refuse refunds the buyer. No names on that screen.

The associate does not

  • See a name, a price, or take cash.
  • Accept a couch, an unboxed item, or anything over 50 pounds.
  • Set a fee, close an account, or photograph the phone.
  • Get a location fee. That slot is $0 on purpose. The bet is foot traffic. It can be turned on later without rewriting checkout. Do not promise a store a dollar.

A buyer or seller can tap “I’m on my way.” That is a button, not a live map of their house. Showing up on time is part of reputation. The store, the buyer, and the seller can each rate the handoff. Thumbs up or down, plus a private comment. A down can be challenged.

Unit economics · a model09

Why the memberships exist

Plus removes the buyer fee. +++ is for the person who sells often and wants the tools.

Standard

Free to join. 5% when they buy. Seller pays $2.99 or 12%. Every sale date is $2.99. They can favorite anything.

Plus · $9.99

Buyer fee is $0, so Plus pays for itself after about $200 of buying in a month. Five free sale days. Alerts for categories, handoffs, or upcoming in-person sales they pick. ID Verified is included when that check is on. Seller fee drops to 8.5%, still with the $2.99 floor.

+++ · $39.99

Everything in Plus. Seller fee 6%. Unlimited sale days, no item cap. Five researcher asks and five “show me their lowest price” reveals a month. Extra researches can be bought one at a time. After a sale, they can see unsold items in overtime. Before a sale starts, they can see items being prepared, with no price.

A seller who does not join still sells. The membership is not a toll to list. It is how a frequent buyer stops paying 5%, and how a frequent seller buys a lower percent, more sale days, and help figuring out what a thing is.

Side lines11

Partner pay · private

Two roles. Paid monthly. No cap. Not a job, and not on the website.

Market lead · invitation only

Kirstin, Diane, Erin. Paid only on people they personally signed, and on an official store they signed.

  • Signed one side: 5% of the fees Rummlee kept on that whole sale. Buyer fee, seller fee, and any store fee. Not the item price. Not tax.
  • Signed both: 10% of that same pool, once. On an $85 Standard sale that is about $1.45, not 72 cents.
  • Plus they signed, in a month that was actually billed: $2.50 that month. A yearly plan is twelve of those, not a $40 check.
  • Official store they signed: $500 once at 50 completed handoffs, $2,000 once at 500. Signing the store does not pay them on strangers who use the counter.

Ambassador · the role that keeps growing

$25 once, when someone they signed has generated $25 that Rummlee kept. After that, later sales pay the ambassador nothing.

No cap on how many people, because each $25 is paid only after that $25 was collected. If a lead already signed that person, the ambassador gets nothing on them.

What never pays: signing another lead, an empty account, a percent of a whole city, two referrers on one person, equity, or a store’s dollar. The scout role is gone.

Stage15

The books, in plain language

Money we are holding is not money we earned. The export already separates them.

  • Held for the buyer until pickup. Not revenue.
  • Owed to the seller after both confirm, then held about 48 hours. Not revenue.
  • Sales tax sits in its own pile for the handoff city. Not revenue.
  • Charity on a left-behind resale sits in its own pile. Not revenue. The charity can be national.
  • Plus paid for a year is earned across the year, not all in the month they paid.
  • Referral pay is a private payable. It is not a public page.
  • Every practice transaction is marked test mode. Do not import those into the real books.

What is not done

No sales-tax permits. No live Stripe. No insurance policy you can hand a store. No signed contractor agreement for a lead. The bookkeeper has an export and a note. They do not have a finished chart of accounts from us. They map our names to their codes.

If a state treats us as the seller of record for local sales, that is a permit and a filing, not a sentence on the website. That question is for the accountant and a lawyer, not for a city lead.

Money16

What can go wrong

The idea is simple. The operations are not.

  • Nobody shows after they pay. We refund. The seller is angry. The store is a closet. Five days is the hold.
  • The store loses a box. Today the buyer is refunded. That sentence is not an insurance policy.
  • A sealed box is not what the listing said. A clerk can refuse what they can see. A locker, later, cannot. Stores first.
  • A city with no sellers. An empty shelf teaches the store that this is a favor. Density is a city problem. Fargo first, then the next city, not three cities on the same Saturday.
  • A name or a street on a public card. That is a different failure than a bug. Privacy is the product.
  • A lead is promised a cut of the lamp. The percent is of our fee. $1.45 on an $85 sale is the both-sides number. Say that before the percentage.
  • We tell a store the traffic is enough, and it is not. The fee table can add a store line. Do not invent the number in a meeting.
  • Real cards turn on before the books, the tax permits, and a lost-package rule. That is the order. Not the other way around.
