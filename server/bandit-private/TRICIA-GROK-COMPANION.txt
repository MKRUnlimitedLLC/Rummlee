WHAT RUMMLEE IS
================================================================================

Rummlee is a local marketplace for things that should not ship. A person lists before the weekend. A buyer pays the asking price or makes one offer. The money is held until both sides confirm the handoff. Nothing ships.

Handoff, in this order:
  1. Official partner store. First. This is the main door.
  2. Public place. Backup.
  3. In person. Optional. The seller turns it on. The address is hidden until someone pays.

Privacy is the product. A handle, not a legal name, on the listing. No home address on Browse, on the item, or on the sale card. The counter never sees a name, a price, or an address. A camera reads a code and does not save the picture.

The buyer we speak to first is a woman in a city or a suburb who will not put her house on Facebook. The marketplace still serves everyone.

Entity: Rummlee Corp, a North Dakota C-corporation. Do not invent other entities.

Stage, as of 24 September 2026:
  rummlee.com is up. Beta. A banner says testing. No real items are the point of the banner.
  Checkout uses test credits. Cards are off. A Stripe key must not turn charges on.
  iOS has been uploaded toward TestFlight. Android is the same app in a shell.
  First city to operate: Fargo–Moorhead. Erin’s city is Minneapolis–St. Paul. Diane’s is Chicago.
  There is no GameStop agreement. A deck exists for Larry Cheng at Volition. It is a proposal, not a deal.

A SALE
================================================================================

The seller opens a Neighborhood sale. The database may still say garage. Never say porch, driveway, or garage-sale app.

Online window: default Tuesday through Thursday. Offers and pay-asking are open. She can change the days.
Live window: optional. Example Saturday 9 to 3. Public card shows hours and a rough distance. Not a street.
A sale is at most 14 days.
Each date is $2.99. Plus includes 5 sale days a month. +++ sale days are free. A single sale still cannot run past 14 days.
Listing an item is free. Minimum asking is $5.

If she turns on “pickup at this sale”:
  Online stays open during the in-person hours.
  A paid item can be picked up at the event. The address shows only after pay.
  When an item sells online, it is held immediately so a second person cannot buy it.
  The seller takes it off the table, sets it aside, and only then taps “I set this aside.”
  The card then says sold online, set aside.

After the sale, unsold items stay in inventory until she removes them or stashes them. Stash means sell later.
+++ members can be offered unsold items in overtime. The seller sets a get-rid-of-it price. One more offer. Not shown to anyone else.
+++ members can see items being prepared, with no price, before the sale starts. That is an early look, not a purchase.

Large items: over 50 pounds, or not in an outer box, are in person only. Not an official store. A couch is the example. No shipping. The buyer may offer to haul it. The seller may offer pickup. The address still waits until pay.

Packing: the seller says as-is, or in an outer box. An outer box is the preference. It is part of how the other person rates them.

================================================================================
THE OFFER
================================================================================

The seller sets the asking price. The seller sets a lowest price. The lowest price is hidden.
The buyer pays asking, or offers lower.
Each side gets one counteroffer. A decline ends it. No long haggling.
Pay asking stays available while the item is live.
One account per identity. Ratings do not reset because someone opens a new account. Support can reset access. Support does not reset the score.

================================================================================
THE HANDOFF
================================================================================

Two different codes.
  Account code: on the phone. Hidden until they tap it. It hides again. It is the person. A photo of it is as good as the phone. Do not put it on the box.
  Item code: short, written or printed on that box. One box, one code. It does not open the account.

Official store:
  Seller brings the box. Counter scans the seller’s phone, then matches the short code.
  Store holds it. Seller has 2 days to drop it off or the buyer is refunded.
  Buyer has 5 days after scan-in, then a refund if it sits.
  Buyer arrives. Counter scans the buyer’s phone. The screen lists the codes to pull. The buyer enters the code on that box. Each box is confirmed on its own.
  Several items from the same seller share the account scan and keep separate item codes.
  Refuse at the counter refunds the buyer. No names on that screen.
  Scanning the code on the screen is the default. Typing is only if the camera is broken.
  Later idea, not built as hardware: a store scanner with a display, then lockers. Same rule. One door, one box, after the account scan.

In person: both confirm with a scan or a 6-character code. “I’m on my way” is a button, not a live map that publishes the house. Showing up on time is a reputation mark.

Escrow releases about 48 hours after both confirm. Not before.

The store is not paid a location fee. That slot is $0 on purpose. Foot traffic is the reason for the store in version one. A per-handoff store payment can be turned on later without a new product. Do not promise a store a check.

Abandoned and left-behind, as designed, not all of it charged yet:
  If a buyer does not pick up, the current product refunds the buyer. Do not describe a 50% abandonment fee. It is not in the product.
  A seller can choose to pick a rejected or unclaimed item back up, or leave it.
  Left at the store, it can become a Rummlee shelf item. Half of what Rummlee receives on that resale is for charity. Charity is not required to be a Fargo charity. It can be national.
  The Fargo shelf is first because that is where a box can actually be stored.
